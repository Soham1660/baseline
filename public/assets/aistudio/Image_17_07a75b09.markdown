# BASELINE — Product Design Specification

**Version:** 1.0  
**Product:** BASELINE  
**Platform:** Responsive web application (desktop + mobile)  
**Tagline:** *Your apartment. Your evidence.*  
**Purpose:** Authoritative design reference for Google Stitch, AI Studio, and frontend implementation.

---

## 1. Product vision

BASELINE helps renters document a property's visible condition before moving in, review timestamped evidence extracted from a walkthrough video, compare that evidence with footage at move-out, and prepare evidence-based responses to disputed deposit deductions.

**Positioning:** A calm, consumer-first evidence instrument, not a landlord property-management dashboard, generic AI assistant, legal-tech portal, or analytics product.

**Core promise:** Make footage easier to inspect and organize. Do **not** imply that the model can establish liability, prove authenticity, guarantee a refund, or identify everything that exists outside the footage.

**The defining experience:** A renter selects a proposed deduction, immediately sees relevant move-in and move-out video frames and timestamps, and can review a carefully qualified evidence assessment. This is **Claim-to-Evidence Matching**.

### Principles

1. **Evidence first.** Footage, source timestamps, and user-reviewed observations are always more prominent than AI-generated language.
2. **Clarity over decoration.** Every element should help a renter record, review, compare, or export evidence.
3. **Human verification.** Findings are reviewable; uncertain outputs remain uncertain.
4. **Privacy by default.** Recordings and reports are private until intentionally shared.
5. **Effortless capture, precise review.** Mobile prioritizes camera capture; desktop prioritizes detailed inspection.
6. **Trustworthy statements.** Distinguish AI suggestions from user-confirmed observations and document-backed evidence.

---

## 2. Brand and visual identity

### Brand language

- **Wordmark:** `BASELINE`, tracked uppercase, charcoal. Do not include a generic sparkle or robot icon.
- **Voice:** Direct, composed, specific, reassuring without exaggeration.
- **Visual metaphor:** Editorial records, film inspection timelines, architectural plans, and modern evidence documentation.
- **Primary surfaces:** Warm paper white, soft ivory, subtle stone. Avoid sterile pure-white everywhere.
- **Accent:** Vermilion red exclusively for findings, attention, and selected evidence markers. Muted green for user-confirmed pre-existing conditions; neither color replaces words.

Use semantic colors sparingly. Normal navigation, buttons, and data should generally remain neutral.

### Typography

- **Editorial/display:** `DM Serif Display` or a comparable refined serif for hero headlines and high-level screen titles; fall back to Georgia.
- **Interface/body:** `Inter` (or system sans-serif) for dense UI, controls, captions, tables, and metadata.
- **Monospace:** `IBM Plex Mono` or system monospace for timestamps, hashes, and file metadata.
- **Display headline:** 56–72px desktop; 36–44px mobile, normal weight, tight leading.
- **Page title:** 32–40px desktop; 27–32px mobile.
- **Section title:** 19–24px.
- **Body:** 14–16px with comfortable line height (1.45–1.6).
- **Metadata:** 12–13px, readable contrast.
- Do not use all-caps for long passages. Reserve uppercase for wordmark, kicker labels, and short document headings.

### Geometry, rhythm, and depth

- **Spacing scale:** 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 40, 48, 64, 80px.
- **Desktop page gutters:** 32–48px; **mobile:** 16–20px.
- **Main panel radius:** 12px; **controls:** 8px; **small badges:** 6px.
- **Borders:** 1px neutral hairlines.
- **Shadows:** Minimal; only for overlays, menus, or layered document previews.
- **Motion:** 150–220ms ease-out for view changes and disclosures; respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Never use glowing gradients, glassmorphism, emoji navigation, floating AI orbs, giant cards, or decorative charts.
